title: Sensors and transistors
unlock: freezer-alarm
para: A sensor in a divider gives a voltage that follows the light or the warmth. Put the middle of that divider on a transistor's base, and the transistor turns the voltage into on or off: below about 0.6 V it stays off, a little above it is fully on.
para: Which way round decides when it switches on. With the LDR below the base, darkness raises its resistance and with it the base: a light that comes on at dusk. With a thermistor above the base, warmth lowers its resistance and raises the base: an alarm that comes on when it warms.
para: The fixed resistor sets where it switches. Larger above the base, or smaller below it, and the sensor must go further before the transistor comes on.
para: Click LDR1 here to change the light on it.
source: Tony R. Kuphaldt, Lessons in Electric Circuits, volume III (Semiconductors), section 4.2, The bipolar junction transistor as a switch: a sensor (a solar cell there) driving the base switches the load.
